# Proposal: push-neon-playtest-polish-roadmap

## Why
The core loop now has a broad boost-card pool, visible passive cards, and stronger draft presentation. The next quality jump should come from playtest-driven polish: clearer combat reads, better enemy identity, stronger wave variety, more satisfying boss beats, and post-run hooks that make another attempt feel obvious.

This change is intentionally a roadmap-sized pass. Each phase should be shippable on its own, but all phases share the same goal: make runs easier to read, easier to tune, and more compelling to replay.

## What Changes
- Add a debug playtest surface for forcing boosts, waves, bosses, and representative chaotic builds.
- Give enemy attacks a consistent telegraph language by threat type instead of one generic warning style.
- Audit boost effects in isolation and in stacked late-run builds, then tune damage, cooldowns, radii, visual weight, and audio.
- Make each enemy archetype more recognizable through silhouette, color, motion, windup, damage, and death feedback.
- Introduce a lightweight run director that creates named combat situations, pressure spikes, and short breathers.
- Polish the Overseer fight with clearer phase identity, warning language, break-window feedback, and finale spectacle.
- Improve juice across hits, dashes, pickups, kills, card casts, camera response, and audio without hurting readability.
- Upgrade post-run panels into a one-more-run loop with build recap, death reason, near-record callouts, and short objectives.

## Success Criteria
- A developer can force any major card or combat situation without playing a full run.
- In screenshots, enemy attack intent is readable without relying on text labels.
- Heavy boost builds remain legible during late waves.
- Enemies can be identified by silhouette and windup before they hit the player.
- Runs alternate between authored pressure patterns rather than feeling like flat spawn scaling.
- Boss phases and break windows are understandable while still feeling spectacular.
- Post-run output clearly explains what happened and gives the player a concrete next target.
